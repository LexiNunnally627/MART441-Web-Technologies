Conceptual Understanding
What was the most important thing you learned about working in 3D space with Three.js? How is this different from working in 2D (Canvas or Phaser)?

Working in 3D has always been a challenge for me, both in physical spaces and digital ones. Something I really struggled with here is getting my camera in the correct spot to see everything. I tried so many different ways to make my camera face ‘upright’ in comparison to my dog model. However, after multiple attempts, I finally got it to a place where I liked it. Then I found out I could zoom in my camera on my website, which made me feel a bit better of where I had it placed. I think coding wise, I like a 2D space better. It’s just easier for my brain to comprehend, I already struggle with coding, so why make it any harder on myself. 

Technical Challenges
What was the most challenging part of this project (camera positioning, lighting, model loading, scale, rotation, debugging)? How did you work through it? Did you use Gen AI? How and why?

Like I mentioned above, I really struggled with my camera positioning. I also struggled to get the correct files in place to make my elements work (ie: OBJLoader). I got to the point where I found it easier to just use the link provided in the lecture material. Of which it works now, it wasn’t before that. I did use some AI to help me troubleshoot my issues. When something wouldn’t work, I’d ask it if it could find anything wrong with my code. It’s nice that it can find my mistakes super fast. 

Design & Intent
Describe the intention behind your scene. What were you trying to create visually or experientially? How did your use of shapes, motion, and models support that goal?

For my scene, I knew I wanted to use my model of my dog that I made for another class about a week ago. It was great that I could use it again for another project. I first followed the lecture material to get my square and lines, and from there my story evolved. I then added my dog model on top of the square, kind of like he is on a rolling pedestal. Then I added a ball, because he loves playing fetch. It also helps that the hex code used in the lecture material was green, and that's the color my family uses to represent him.
